{% comment %} The composer — the primary surface of the whole application, and since the approved Teema target **open on arrival**. Two text boxes, a row of quick dates with a file affordance at its right end, five progressive panels and one `Salvesta`. Everything a lawyer records after a meeting goes in here and saves once: what happened, what happens next, the file they were handed, the deadline they were told about, the consultation that informed it, the win, the commencement, and — when the work is finished — the closure itself. **OPEN BY DEFAULT.** This reverses the 2026-08 decision to close it (ADR 0043). That decision was reasonable on its own terms — a Matter is read far more often than written to, and an eight-line form pushed the file's content below the fold. What the approved target does instead is make the form *short enough to live open*: the body is a 60 px box rather than eight lines, the next step is one row, and everything else is a chip. Recording what happened is the reason this product exists, and it must not begin with a click (TEEMA_TARGET_SPEC §C.2, docs/adr/0074 §3). The collapsed presentation is kept, because the disclosure still closes — by the `L` shortcut, by the summary, and by clicking the Järgmiseks row above. **One save.** The POST goes to one service inside one transaction; it may write eight canonical records and the chronology shows what a person did, not what the application did (app/matters/services.py `compose_update`, app/audit/operations). **Three primary questions, in the order a lawyer thinks them.** *Mida tegid või mis juhtus?*, *Järgmiseks*, *Millal?* — and nothing else is asked about the next step. There is no action kind and no date meaning: the stored values behind a step recorded here are always `DO` / `DEADLINE` / `EXACT`, and the date means the day the work gets done (ADR 0052). **The two text boxes are two facts.** What happened and what happens next are written separately and stored separately, and neither is derived from the other — no splitting, no summarising, no copying (reversing ADR 0030 §4). `.composer` stays on the element beside `.uxcomp`: the focus treatment for the textarea is `.composer:focus-within`, and dropping the class would leave the field with no visible focus at all (static/css/app.css). {% endcomment %} {% comment %} `open` unconditionally. Not «open unless…», and not «open when a save was refused» — that was the old rule and it is the one the target replaces. A refused save therefore comes back open for the same reason an ordinary load does, and nobody loses what they typed either way. {% endcomment %}
